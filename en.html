About

Research

  • Fondecyt de Iniciación

    ANID · Principal Investigator · Since 2025

    Preference-based measurement of well-being. This project develops methods to measure well-being based on how people value dimensions of their lives, such as health and income. It uses the concept of equivalent income to compare well-being and examine inequalities associated with circumstances beyond individual control.

    One strand examines how adaptation to life circumstances can shape preferences and affect the measurement of well-being. Another explores equity criteria for assessing lifestyle-related health inequalities.

  • Inequalities in mortality and survival

    As part of my work at CECAN, the RIS Research project links administrative records to study how education and income relate to mortality, life expectancy and cancer survival in Chile.

  • Colorectal cancer screening

    CECAN screening research

    Stanford Impact Labs project

    I am a co-principal investigator (co-PI) on this international collaboration supported by Stanford Impact Labs (SIL). We assess colorectal cancer screening strategies and their cost-effectiveness for the Chilean health system. This work includes assessing colonoscopy capacity and estimating adenoma prevalence.

Working papers

  • Lifestyle preferences and health inequality: Reference-set welfare and allocation choices

    Silva-Illanes N and Tsuchiya A. This manuscript examines how lifestyle preferences and reference standards for measuring well-being affect the assessment of health inequalities.

  • Effort as Disutility and the Reward Principle in Equality of Opportunity: Theory and Evidence from Health-Related Lifestyles

    This manuscript examines what counts as effort in equality-of-opportunity theory and whether people agree on how to rank behaviors by the effort they require.
